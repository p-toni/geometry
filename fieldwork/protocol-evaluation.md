# protocol self-evaluation

Status: **active**

The anti-collapse protocol is itself an experimental object. A process that feels rigorous is not enough; it should eventually outperform simpler research procedures on observable epistemic outcomes.

## Current evaluation question

> Does this protocol produce better calibrated, more discriminating, less collapse-prone research than a simpler deep-research → synthesis baseline?

We do not yet know.

## Leading indicators

Track these during a round:

- **displacement rate** — how often exploration creates a genuinely new distinction, contradiction, discriminator, or changed claim;
- **falsification pressure** — fraction of probes designed to make a live claim fail rather than merely demonstrate a distinction;
- **probe outcome diversity** — answered, failed, ambiguous, bad discriminator, unverifiable;
- **branch independence** — how often fresh-start / alien branches produce material displacement not predicted by the inherited map;
- **compression loss** — whether low-yield branches suppressed from human view later prove important under audit;
- **revision calibration** — whether claims are promoted only after the promised verification threshold;
- **search waste** — how much frontier exploration produces no displacement, recorded as a cost rather than hidden;
- **reconstruction drift** — how much source-closed reconstruction differs from the seed and from source-saturated synthesis.

These are diagnostics, not yet validated metrics.

## Falsification portfolio

After the first source-closed reconstruction, default probe allocation shifts from distinction-demonstration toward adversarial failure:

```text
~30% formalize / demonstrate a distinction
~70% try to break a working claim
```

The ratio is approximate. The key requirement is that a mature probe portfolio should not consist entirely of clean confirmations.

If every probe returns a neat answer in the direction that motivated it, treat that as evidence that the probes may be too easy.

## Random suppression audit

Human-facing compression can hide surprises. Periodically select one paused / low-yield branch by a predeclared mechanical rule, reopen it briefly, and ask whether suppression discarded material displacement.

A good audit can conclude that the branch was correctly suppressed.

## Baseline comparison

At least once per major round, run the same new research question through two procedures:

### A. Simple baseline

```text
research broadly → synthesize best answer
```

No blind scouts, branch roles, collision gating, source-closed reconstruction, or explicit verification fabric.

### B. Anti-collapse protocol

Use the full procedure.

Before comparing outputs, freeze evaluation criteria:

1. number and quality of distinct mechanisms recovered;
2. unsupported universalizations;
3. live rival explanations preserved;
4. explicit failure conditions;
5. discriminating probes proposed;
6. source / inference boundary quality;
7. calibration of uncertainty;
8. useful conclusions per unit of search effort.

Because the same model family may execute both procedures, this tests **procedural effect**, not independent intelligence. It cannot establish external validity by itself.

## Failure conditions for the protocol

The protocol should be weakened, simplified, or abandoned if repeated comparisons show that it:

- mainly creates paperwork without additional displacement;
- preserves alternatives long after evidence should eliminate them;
- produces more distinctions but fewer useful predictions or decisions;
- systematically suppresses high-value branches;
- makes verification contracts decorative rather than outcome-changing;
- cannot outperform a much simpler baseline relative to search cost;
- becomes a universal vocabulary into which every research problem is translated.

The protocol does not receive immunity from its own anti-collapse rule.
