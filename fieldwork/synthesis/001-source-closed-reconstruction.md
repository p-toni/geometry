This note is written from the structure that remained after the first large exploration waves, not by summarizing source cards. It is intentionally allowed to be incomplete. Sources may be reopened later only to correct provenance or overclaiming.

## Questions that exist now that did not exist at the start

1. **Equivalent for what?**  
   Observation, prediction, task performance, intervention, and scale can induce different justified equivalence relations over the same system.

2. **Which kind of constraint?**  
   A representational cut, an admissibility rule, a dynamical attractor, and historical lock-in all reduce apparent possibility differently.

3. **Is the state space given?**  
   Dynamics, boundary conditions, prior choices, and representations that feed back into behavior can change the effective frontier itself.

4. **At what explanatory level is a claimed invariant?**  
   Shared function does not imply shared algorithm; shared macrobehavior does not imply shared mechanism; shared description does not imply shared ontology.

5. **Which boundary is the learner?**  
   Memory, observation, intervention, verification, authority, accountability, and persistence may live at different or overlapping boundaries.

6. **Through what channel does the world answer?**  
   External constraint is not enough. The interface must be sensitive to it, the signal must survive transformation, a verifier must discriminate it, and the result must reach something capable of revision.

7. **Does order imply representation?**  
   No. Coherent order, persistence, convergence, and restoration can arise through several mechanisms that require no internal map or explicit target.

8. **Can a representation change what it represents?**  
   Yes in at least some systems: public summaries, prices, transmitted languages, rules, and other representations can alter incentives or future inputs.

## Distinctions that survived source closure

### 1. Possibility ≠ accessibility ≠ representation ≠ persistence

Something can be possible yet dynamically unreachable, reachable yet unrepresented, represented yet unstable, or persistent while its mechanism changes.

### 2. Partition ≠ constraint

Grouping states in a map is not the same operation as changing which states or transitions can occur.

### 3. Observation ≠ prediction ≠ intervention ≠ control

Access to one does not entail the others. A system can be observable without being controllable, controllable without full observability, or observationally indistinguishable until an intervention changes the information available.

### 4. Stability ≠ resilience ≠ reliability ≠ convergence

These can all produce something that “keeps working,” but through different mechanisms and under different perturbations.

### 5. Structure ≠ ontology

Strong structural regularity does not by itself choose between realism, nominalism, mechanism, dynamics, or a Platonic account.

### 6. Contact ≠ discrimination

The world can constrain a system while the chosen measurement channel still fails to separate competing explanations.

### 7. Verification has layers

A derivation can be valid while the formal statement mismatches the intended claim; the statement can match while the verifier has a trusted-base failure; all of those can pass while no human yet understands the result.

### 8. Boundary is operation-relative

There may be no single useful answer to “where is the system?” until the operation—memory, action, error integration, authority, reproduction, verification—is specified.

## What the frozen seed can no longer say without qualification

Frozen seed:

> A bounded learner cannot carry the whole world.  
> So it searches for invariants.  
> Invariants induce symmetries.  
> Symmetries make compression possible.  
> Compression makes prediction and control possible.  
> Prediction and control only matter if the world is allowed to answer.

### “So it searches for invariants”

Too strong as a universal transition. Some systems acquire useful order through local dynamics, selective constraints, repeated transmission, or distributed interaction without anything that needs to be described as searching for invariants.

### “Invariants induce symmetries”

The direction is not defensible as written. Invariance and symmetry are defined relative to transformations; in some mathematical settings the transformation class is specified first and invariants are then studied. Neither term should silently be made the cause of the other.

### “Symmetries make compression possible”

Sometimes, but not as a general hinge. Useful reduction can be licensed by task relevance, scale, causal informativeness, sufficient statistics, or other structures that are not captured by symmetry alone.

### “Compression makes prediction and control possible”

Too bundled. Prediction and control are distinct capabilities. Compression can destroy what matters for one task while preserving another. The relevant question is what information/structure is preserved, for which operation, under which regime.

### “The world is allowed to answer”

Still load-bearing, but under-specified. Answerability now appears to require a channel: interaction, measurement, transport, verification, and a revision boundary.

## What remains load-bearing

### Boundedness

Still strong. No explored branch required an unbounded learner. What changed is what follows from boundedness: it does not uniquely imply one strategy such as invariant search or compression.

### Selectivity

Still strong, but broader than omission. Bounded systems select by representation, admissibility, dynamics, history, attention, incentives, and interface.

### Answerability

Stronger than at the start, but less primitive. It now has internal structure and failure surfaces.

### Weak maps / delayed closure

The exploration repeatedly benefited from keeping mechanisms distinct even when they produced superficially similar outcomes. Premature unification would have erased most of the useful distinctions above.

## Competing pictures that remain irreducible

### Constructed geometry

Useful structure is produced by bounded systems through cuts, tasks, histories, representations, and repeated interaction.

### Dynamically selected structure

Useful macroscopic form emerges because equations, constraints, energy flow, boundary conditions, or local interaction make some regimes stable/reachable and others not.

### Discovered structure

A learner identifies relations that are not arbitrary consequences of its own representation, even though access to them is interface-dependent.

### Accessed / pre-existing structure

Embodiment or interface may provide access to a structured possibility space not generated by the local system. Nothing in round 001 has verified the stronger ontology required by this picture, but neither has the search eliminated it.

### Distributed structure

The relevant organization may exist at a system/history level that no component individually represents or owns.

No current result forces these pictures into one ontology.

## A candidate weaker backbone — not a seed revision

The minimum statement that currently survives is closer to:

> A bounded system cannot realize, observe, or represent every possibility.  
> Interfaces, constraints, dynamics, and history shape what becomes accessible.  
> Any useful equivalence is indexed by what must be preserved.  
> Structure may be represented, enacted, selected, or distributed.  
> Prediction, intervention, and control require different forms of access.  
> Learning requires that consequential error survive a channel and reach something that can revise.

This is deliberately less elegant than the frozen seed. It carries more distinctions and therefore commits less prematurely.

## Next discriminating probes

1. **same function, different mechanism**  
   Construct two systems with identical input/output behavior but different internal transition structure; test which proposed cross-domain claims remain true after explanatory level is fixed.

2. **observation vs intervention**  
   Build a minimal pair of causal models that are observationally equivalent but diverge under one intervention; use exact enumeration as verifier.

3. **plural boundary trace**  
   Take one distributed system and locate state, observation, error integration, verification, and authority separately. Test whether a single-boundary description loses predictive information.

4. **heterogeneous admissibility check**  
   Compare one formal type-system case, one physical boundary-condition case, and one institutional-rule case. Determine what survives once “constraint” is decomposed by causal role.

5. **contact-channel failure injection**  
   Construct a simple process where the environment changes but a lossy measurement channel makes two conditions indistinguishable; then change only the channel and recover discrimination.

## Round decision

Do **not** revise the seed yet.

The exploration has generated real displacement, but most of the strongest new statements currently have one formal certificate plus conceptual/empirical support rather than heterogeneous verification across independent mechanisms.

Continue the round with a smaller number of discriminating probes and fresh-start exploration at the edges. The next progress should come more from eliminating live alternatives than from accumulating adjacent sources.
