Branch: `organizational-closure`

## Native question

Can a set of chemical reactions become collectively self-sustaining because its members catalyze one another using resources available from an external food set?

## Claims in the source's own terms

RAF theory formalizes reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated reaction sets. A RAF is collectively catalyzed by molecules available within the set and can generate its required reactants from a specified food set. The framework gives algorithms and probabilistic models for asking when such self-sustaining catalytic organization is likely to appear.

Autocatalytic closure is presented as a plausible ingredient in origin-of-life scenarios, not as a sufficient definition of life.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The review summarizes formal RAF theory, computational results, criticisms, and experimental evidence relevant to autocatalytic chemical networks.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- Autocatalytic sets are argued to be important but not sufficient for full living organization.
- Boundary formation, heredity, metabolism, and evolution require additional treatment.
- Spontaneous emergence probabilities depend on model assumptions and chemistry.

## Assumptions under pressure

A self-maintaining organization can be defined relationally across reactions and resources without invoking a represented target form.

## Rival reading

RAF structure may capture only one necessary layer of origin-of-life organization. Real chemistry, spatial compartmentalization, inhibition, energetics, and heredity can dominate behavior that a bare catalytic graph misses.

## Possible displacement of the seed

No direct displacement yet. It provides a native mechanism for self-sustaining closure that can compete with stronger goal-state or pattern-space explanations of persistence.
