# source — Wilson & Kogut 1974: the renormalization group and the epsilon expansion

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Kenneth G. Wilson & John Kogut, “The Renormalization Group and the ε Expansion,” *Physics Reports* 12(2), 1974, 75–199.  
Branch: `coarse-graining-and-universality`

## Native question

How can critical behavior be understood across scales when microscopic details differ strongly between systems?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Renormalization-group analysis studies how a system's effective description changes as short-distance degrees of freedom are successively integrated out and parameters are transformed.

Near critical points, many microscopically different systems can flow toward the same fixed-point behavior. This underwrites universality: certain large-scale properties depend on a restricted set of relevant features while many microscopic differences become irrelevant under repeated scale transformation.

The language of relevant and irrelevant operators is not ordinary importance language; relevance is defined by how perturbations grow or shrink under the renormalization flow.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The review develops the mathematical and physical machinery of renormalization for critical phenomena and quantum field theory, including recursion relations, fixed points, scaling behavior, and the epsilon expansion.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- Universality is conditional. Not all details wash out, and which variables are relevant depends on the fixed point and problem.
- Coarse-graining can destroy information required for questions outside the macroscopic regime being studied.
- The framework does not imply that every useful abstraction is an RG transformation.

## Assumptions under pressure

Ignoring a distinction can be physically licensed rather than merely convenient when repeated scale transformations demonstrably drive its influence toward zero for the phenomenon of interest.

## Rival reading

Universality can tempt over-generalization. Similar macroscopic exponents do not make systems identical in mechanism, history, transient behavior, or response away from the critical regime.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** an “allowed cut” may sometimes be earned by a transformation process showing that a degree of freedom is irrelevant at a specified scale. This is stronger than observer-chosen omission. It also reinforces the need to specify the transformation class and regime before naming an invariant.
