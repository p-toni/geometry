# source — Britton et al. 2002: how honeybees agree

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: N. F. Britton, N. R. Franks, S. C. Pratt & T. D. Seeley, “Deciding on a new home: how do honeybees agree?”, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 269 (2002), 1383–1388. DOI: 10.1098/rspb.2002.2001. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12079662/  
Branch: `collective-choice-without-comparison`

## Native question

Can a honeybee swarm reach one nest-site decision without any individual bee comparing all candidate sites?

## Claims in the source's own terms

The authors adapt mathematical models of information/belief spread to nest-site selection and show that colony-level agreement can arise without requiring any bee to perform a global comparison among alternatives. Recruitment dynamics can amplify support until one site dominates.

Related empirical work on bee swarms supports a decentralized picture in which scouts use local information, recruitment and quorum-like mechanisms rather than a central evaluator.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The paper is a mathematical modeling study grounded in observed honeybee nest-site behavior. It demonstrates that a no-global-comparison mechanism can reproduce collective agreement under plausible recruitment dynamics.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- Showing that a distributed mechanism is sufficient does not prove it is the only mechanism bees use.
- Colony decisions still depend on individual site assessment and communication.
- Group-level success does not imply group consciousness, knowledge or understanding.
- Different social insects use different coordination details.

## Assumptions under pressure

A system-level discriminator need not be implemented as a single comparison operation anywhere inside the system.

## Rival reading

One can describe the colony as implementing a comparison in a distributed functional sense, but that redescribes the outcome; it should not erase the mechanistic fact that no component performs the global operation.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** “the system chooses” does not identify where comparison, representation or understanding occurs. Those boundaries can separate even when the collective outcome is highly discriminating.
