# source — Kirby, Cornish & Smith 2008: cumulative cultural evolution in language

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Simon Kirby, Hannah Cornish & Kenny Smith, “Cumulative cultural evolution in the laboratory: An experimental approach to the origins of structure in human language,” *PNAS* 105(31), 2008, 10681–10686. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0707835105. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2504810/  
Branch: `cultural-transmission-and-structure`

## Native question

Can repeated transmission through human learners generate structured communication systems even when the starting system lacks that structure and no participant designs the final result?

## Claims in the source's own terms

The experiments use diffusion chains in which participants learn and reproduce artificial languages that are then passed to subsequent learners.

Across transmission, the languages become easier to learn and increasingly structured. The authors interpret this as experimental evidence that cultural transmission can generate apparent design without a designer: languages adapt to the pressures imposed by being repeatedly learned and transmitted.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The paper reports controlled laboratory experiments with artificial languages transmitted across chains of participants. Changes in learnability and structural regularity are measured across generations.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- Artificial-language tasks are simplified relative to natural-language evolution.
- Learner biases and communicative pressures can interact; transmission alone is not the only force relevant to real languages.
- The experiment does not imply that structure always increases under bottlenecks; particular task conditions matter.

## Assumptions under pressure

A bottleneck does not merely destroy information. Repeated passage through bounded learners can systematically reshape what survives, producing structure at the population/history level that no participant explicitly planned.

## Rival reading

The effect may reflect the specific inductive biases and task design of human participants rather than a general law of cultural systems. The emergent structure should therefore not be generalized beyond demonstrated transmission regimes without further evidence.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** boundedness can be generative across iterations. The relevant structure may arise from a sequence of lossy reconstructions rather than from one learner discovering a pre-existing invariant. This creates a serious alternative to a purely individual-level account of geometry formation.
