# source — Chong & Proctor 2020: affordances after Gibson

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Isis Chong & Robert W. Proctor, “On the Evolution of a Radical Concept: Affordances According to Gibson and Their Subsequent Use and Development,” *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 15(1), 2020, 117–132. DOI: 10.1177/1745691619868207.  
Branch: `perception-without-representation`

## Native question

What did Gibson originally mean by direct perception and affordances, and how did later psychology alter that concept?

## Claims in the source's own terms

The review presents Gibson's ecological psychology as a radical alternative to representational perception. On that view, perception is not primarily the construction of an internal picture from impoverished inputs. Organisms can directly pick up structured information in natural environments, including **affordances**: opportunities for action relative to an organism's capabilities.

The authors' historical point is equally important: `affordance` later migrated into several psychological traditions and often changed meaning. Some later “affordance” accounts reintroduced internal representations or stimulus–response mechanisms that differ substantially from Gibson's original organism–environment proposal.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

This is a conceptual and historical review of Gibson and several later experimental traditions, not a single decisive experiment for direct perception. Its contribution is to separate competing uses of one successful term and show that apparently shared vocabulary can hide different explanatory frameworks.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- Direct perception remains theoretically contested.
- The review does not show that internal representation is never needed for cognition.
- Affordance research contains multiple operational definitions; empirical results under one definition cannot automatically support another.
- Naturalistic perception/action is not the same problem as abstract reasoning, mathematics, or long-horizon planning.

## Assumptions under pressure

Competent action does not automatically imply that an organism has first built an explicit internal map of the relevant structure. On the Gibsonian account, some relevant structure is available in the organism–environment relation itself.

## Rival reading

Representational and inferential theories can accept rich environmental structure while still arguing that internal states transform, integrate, predict, or reconstruct that information. Gibson therefore challenges the necessity and explanatory role of representation; he does not make every internal-structure hypothesis impossible.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** “the learner searches for invariants” may conceal two very different possibilities: **constructing/storing an invariant internally** versus **detecting invariance available in ongoing environmental information**. Those should not be merged.

The source also strengthens an anti-collapse warning: shared vocabulary such as `affordance`, `map`, or `geometry` can survive while the underlying theory changes completely.
