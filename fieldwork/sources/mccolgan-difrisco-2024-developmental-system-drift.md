# source — McColgan & DiFrisco 2024: developmental system drift

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Áine McColgan & James DiFrisco, “Understanding developmental system drift,” *Development* 151(20), 2024. DOI: 10.1242/dev.203054. https://journals.biologists.com/dev/article/151/20/dev203054/362393/Understanding-developmental-system-drift  
Branch: `morphogenesis-and-persistence`

## Native question

How can homologous traits remain phenotypically conserved while the genetic mechanisms that produce them diverge over evolutionary time?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Developmental system drift (DSD) is divergence in the genetic basis of a homologous trait despite conservation of the phenotype. The review argues that DSD may be pervasive across organisms and developmental processes, although its frequency is not yet known.

The authors distinguish DSD from both genetic drift and robustness. Robustness is a property of a developmental system; DSD is an evolutionary outcome. Robustness can permit molecular changes to accumulate without changing the phenotype, but DSD can also arise through compensatory evolution after changes elsewhere disturb a correlated developmental process.

A conserved phenotype therefore does not license the inference that its developmental genetic architecture is conserved.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

This is a review, not a single experiment. It surveys evidence for DSD across cases including vertebrate segmentation, nematode vulval development, and insect gap-gene networks, and separates proposed mechanisms such as mutational robustness and compensatory evolution. It also proposes observational, perturbational, and computational strategies for detecting DSD and estimating its frequency.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- The exact frequency of DSD is unknown.
- Evo-devo remains heavily dependent on a small set of model organisms, which can hide lineage-specific divergence.
- Establishing DSD requires establishing trait homology; convergent traits with different mechanisms are not DSD.
- The review does not claim that conserved form requires a represented target, an attractor, or any non-physical source.

## Assumptions under pressure

The relevant “same thing” is chosen at a level: a phenotype can count as conserved while its molecular implementation changes substantially. That makes level-of-description part of the empirical claim, not just presentation.

## Rival reading

DSD can be explained within standard evolutionary-developmental resources: mutation, robustness, redundancy, stabilizing selection, pleiotropy, and compensation. Stable phenotype with changing mechanism is therefore not evidence for a latent pattern-space or a goal state by itself.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** when the seed says “invariant” or “persistent,” it must specify *at what level and under which transformations*. Surface or phenotypic invariance can coexist with causal drift underneath. A useful invariant may therefore be a relation among changing mechanisms rather than preservation of the mechanism itself.
