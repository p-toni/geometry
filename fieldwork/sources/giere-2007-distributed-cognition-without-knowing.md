# source — Giere 2007: distributed cognition without distributed knowing

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Ronald N. Giere, “Distributed Cognition without Distributed Knowing,” *Social Epistemology* 21(3), 2007, 313–320. DOI: 10.1080/02691720701674197. https://doi.org/10.1080/02691720701674197  
Branch: `distributed-knowing`

## Native question

If a socio-technical system performs cognitive work across people and artifacts, does it follow that the system itself knows, believes, has intentions, or is an epistemic agent?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Giere accepts distributed cognition as a useful description of many scientific and technical systems while resisting the further move to distributed *knowing*. Humans, instruments, records, and procedures can jointly transform and propagate representations without requiring that the whole system be treated as a mind or knowing subject.

He therefore separates a distributed cognitive process from full epistemic agency. The institution or system can reliably produce knowledge without needing to be attributed beliefs, consciousness, expectations, or responsibility in the same sense as a person.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The paper is a philosophical analysis drawing on distributed-cognition cases in science and Hutchins-style socio-technical systems. Giere uses examples such as large scientific instrumentation to show how quickly the boundary and agency questions become strained if every causal component is absorbed into one knowing mind.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- This is an argument about warranted attribution, not an experimental demonstration that groups cannot know.
- Collective epistemology offers rival accounts under which groups can sometimes qualify as epistemic subjects.
- The line between causal participation, cognitive participation, and epistemic agency remains theory-dependent.

## Assumptions under pressure

A system can have reliable information-processing properties that no individual possesses without that fact settling who, if anyone, should be called the knower.

## Rival reading

Group-agency and collective-knowledge theories can argue that appropriately organized systems meet stronger criteria for epistemic agency. Giere's caution blocks an inference; it does not prove individualism.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** split the boundary question. Ask separately **where transformation occurs**, **where error is integrated**, **where verification is performed**, **who is entitled to the resulting belief**, and **where responsibility lands**. These boundaries may not coincide.
