# source — Longo, Montévil & Kauffman: unprestatable phase spaces in evolution

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Giuseppe Longo, Maël Montévil, Stuart Kauffman, “No entailing laws, but enablement in the evolution of the biosphere,” arXiv:1201.2069 (2012); related development in Longo & Montévil, “Extended criticality, phase spaces and enablement in biology” (2013).  
Branch / attack: `unprestatable-phase-space`

## Native question

Can biological evolution be represented as motion through a fixed, prestated phase space in the way physical dynamics normally is?

## Claims in the source's own terms

The authors argue that in biological evolution the relevant observables, phenotypes, niches, and boundary conditions change in ways that cannot be prestated in advance. On their account, the phase space itself changes historically rather than merely containing unknown future trajectories.

They distinguish this from ordinary physical uncertainty: the problem is not only which state will occur, but which variables / possibilities will later become scientifically pertinent. They introduce `enablement` to describe conditions that make later possibilities available without entailing them by fixed equations of motion.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The paper is a theoretical argument built from contrasts between physical phase spaces and biological evolution, with examples involving changing phenotypes, niches, ecological interactions, and symmetry changes.

It is not a theorem showing that all biological phase spaces are formally impossible to construct.

## Assumptions / commitments

- biologically relevant observables are historically generated;
- novelty in evolution is not reducible to uncertainty over a fixed list of predefined states;
- the physics-style law-entailment framework is not sufficient for diachronic biological evolution.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- The “unprestatability” thesis is contested and difficult to verify directly.
- Local or restricted evolutionary models can still use fixed state spaces effectively.
- The argument does not imply that all state-space methods in biology are useless.

## Strongest rival reading

A sufficiently expressive formal model might treat apparent novelty as movement in a very large latent state space or allow dynamic model expansion. On that reading, “unprestatable” reflects epistemic limits rather than a principled impossibility.

## Possible displacement

**Hostile pressure on the research metaphor:** state-space exploration may be too static even after adding path dependence and effective-frontier bias. Some inquiry may require invention of new coordinates / observables, not merely discovery of states in an existing frontier.

The protocol should therefore distinguish:

- unknown location in a known state space;
- changing reachability within a known state space;
- changing transition rules;
- emergence/invention of new dimensions or observables.

This is a protocol correction, not evidence for a biological ontology of unprestatability.
