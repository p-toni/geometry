# source — Onuchic, Luthey-Schulten & Wolynes 1997: energy landscape perspective

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: José N. Onuchic, Zaida Luthey-Schulten & Peter G. Wolynes, “Theory of Protein Folding: The Energy Landscape Perspective,” *Annual Review of Physical Chemistry* 48 (1997), 545–600. DOI: 10.1146/annurev.physchem.48.1.545. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9348663/  
Branch: `folding-landscapes`

## Native question

How can protein folding be understood statistically when the number of possible conformations is enormous and folding does not proceed through one compulsory sequence of intermediates?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Energy-landscape theory describes folding as motion over an ensemble of conformations. Foldable protein-like heteropolymers have landscapes that are rugged but globally biased toward the native structure: a **funnel**. The theory connects this bias to minimal frustration, where energetic conflicts are sufficiently reduced that many microscopic routes can still lead toward the folded state.

The framework therefore replaces exhaustive conformational search and one-path descriptions with a statistical landscape whose structure makes some regions and transitions far more accessible than others.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The review develops statistical-mechanical theory, compares homopolymers, random heteropolymers, and protein-like heteropolymers, and relates the theory to simulations, fast-folding experiments, and structure prediction.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- A funnel is not literally smooth or single-path; real landscapes contain barriers, traps, and alternative ensembles.
- Cellular folding can depend on chaperones, co-translational processes, environment, and function-specific constraints.
- Intrinsically disordered or strongly multi-state proteins do not fit a simple single-native-state story.
- The account is physicochemical; it does not posit a learner searching a conceptual space.

## Assumptions under pressure

The practical difficulty of a state space depends on its transition structure, energetic bias, and history, not merely on the number of possible states.

## Rival reading

Calling the landscape “funnel-shaped” can become explanatory shorthand unless the energy function and kinetics actually predict measurable folding behavior. The useful content is quantitative bias and frustration, not the visual metaphor.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** a bounded search process may succeed because the state space is already dynamically shaped. The relevant object is not only what possibilities exist, but **which transitions are probable, reachable, and costly**.
