## Native question

Can the apparent gap between possibility and embodied form be explained without a separate Platonic domain?

## Candidate sequence proposed

> potential → distinction → admissible possibility → selection → closure → persistent form

Smart proposes that the space of possible forms may be unactualised structure of the same underlying field rather than a second realm. Physical structure and boundary conditions determine accessibility; dynamics select among admissible configurations; closure stabilizes one into persistent actuality.

In that frame, what Levin calls ingression could instead be described as selection and closure within one reality:

> possibility ↔ actuality

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The supplied statement is a conceptual proposal. It does not provide a formal model, experiment, or discriminating result in the text we have.

## Assumptions under pressure

- 'underlying field' requires specification before it explains more than possibility-space language;
- distinction, admissibility, selection, and closure need operational definitions;
- the account must show what observations would differ from ordinary dynamical-systems or constraint-based descriptions.

## Rival reading

This may be a useful re-description of constrained dynamics rather than a new ontology. That would still matter if the sequence yields measurable variables or better interventions.

## Possible displacement of the seed

It gives **closure** a positive role: not only premature epistemic finality, but the stabilization needed for a form to persist. That distinction is potentially real and should not be collapsed.
