# source — Amari 2021: Information Geometry

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Shun-ichi Amari, “Information Geometry,” *International Statistical Review* 89(2), 2021. DOI: 10.1111/insr.12464.  
Branch: `tetrahedral-synergetics`

## Native question

What does it mean, in a mature mathematical field, for information/statistical models to have geometry?

## Claims in the source's own terms

A parameterized family of probability distributions forms a statistical manifold. Its intrinsic local geometry can be described using the Fisher–Rao information metric, with further structure supplied by dual affine connections and divergences.

The geometry is not a literal spatial shape carried by individual data. Points are probability distributions; distances, curvature, connections, and geodesics encode distinguishability and inferential structure among distributions.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The field develops differential-geometric structure from statistical models and shows that this structure supports results and applications in inference, signal processing, machine learning, and related areas.

A central invariant object is the Fisher information metric, historically originating with Rao and developed extensively by Amari and others.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- “information has geometry” here is domain-specific: it means a family of probability distributions admits mathematically useful geometric structure;
- different statistical model families can have different dimensions, curvature, and connection structure;
- nothing in information geometry identifies one universal polyhedral shape as the geometry of all information;
- a manifold may be triangulated or embedded using simplices without that triangulation becoming its intrinsic metric geometry.

## Rival reading

The existence of an intrinsic statistical geometry may still support the broader intuition that stable relational structure, rather than raw symbols, is important for information. But that intuition is weaker than claiming a unique geometry or stable shape rules every information-bearing system.

## Possible displacement

**Candidate displacement:** “geometry of information” has a rigorous interpretation, but it is relational and model-indexed rather than a claim that information literally settles into one universal shape. This is a control against converting the phrase “geometry is the stable shape of information” directly into ontology.
