# source — Prigogine 1977: dissipative structures

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Ilya Prigogine, Nobel Lecture, 1977, on non-equilibrium thermodynamics and dissipative structures. https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/prigogine-lecture.pdf  
Branch: `dissipative-order-boundary-conditions`

## Native question

How can ordered macroscopic structures arise in systems maintained far from thermodynamic equilibrium, and what controls which structures appear?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Prigogine distinguishes equilibrium structures from **dissipative structures** that exist only through continuing exchanges of matter and energy with the environment. Far from equilibrium, nonlinear systems can undergo bifurcations into new steady or oscillatory regimes.

The lecture emphasizes that the resulting structures can depend decisively on global environmental features: system size, geometry, and boundary conditions can alter which instabilities and branches become available.

Fluctuations are not merely noise around a predetermined state; near instabilities they can trigger transitions into different macroscopic structures.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The lecture synthesizes mathematical stability/bifurcation analysis with chemical reaction–diffusion models and experimental phenomena such as oscillating chemical reactions and spatial pattern formation.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- The formal results concern specified thermodynamic and chemical systems; they do not establish a universal law of cognition or organization.
- Later nonequilibrium statistical mechanics provides additional formalisms and should not be collapsed into Prigogine's broader philosophical language.
- Dependence on boundary conditions does not imply that the environment uniquely determines the resulting structure.

## Rival reading

The important lesson may be narrower than “self-organization”: nonlinear driven systems have regime-specific bifurcation structures. Generic metaphors about emergence add little unless they preserve the equations, control parameters, and stability conditions.

## Possible displacement

**Candidate displacement:** the relevant possibility space can be **constituted by dynamics plus boundary conditions**, not merely searched within. Accessibility is therefore sometimes a property of the coupled system-environment regime rather than a fixed background state space.
