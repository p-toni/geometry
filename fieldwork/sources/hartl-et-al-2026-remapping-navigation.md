# source — Hartl et al. 2026, *Remapping and navigation of an embedding space via error minimization*

Journal: *Physics of Life Reviews* 58, 336–359

## Native question

Can cognition across biological and artificial systems be characterized by substrate-independent organizational invariants?

## Claims in the source's own frame

The authors propose two broad invariants: systems remap embedding spaces and navigate within them, with iterative/self-regulatory error minimization connecting the two. They survey cells, tissues, organisms, transformers, diffusion models, world models, neural cellular automata, and evolution.

The paper treats embedding spaces broadly: they need not be smooth, continuous, or Euclidean, and can be discrete, graph-like, hybrid, or otherwise effective relational spaces.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The paper is synthetic and comparative. It gathers examples from neuroscience, developmental biology, machine learning, and dynamical systems, and provides mathematical language for coherent remappings, coarse-graining, nested scales, and navigation toward goal states.

## Explicit limits

The authors do not claim that biological and artificial systems instantiate identical mathematical objects. They explicitly distinguish current transformers from living, lifelong world-modeling agents and warn against directly equating artificial morphogenetic models with biology.

## Assumptions under pressure

The framework may be too permissive if almost any persistent adaptive system can be redescribed as navigating/remapping an embedding space while minimizing error. The important test is what the proposed invariants rule out.

## Rival reading

The useful weakened claim may be methodological rather than ontological: many adaptive systems can be profitably modeled using remapping/navigation language even if there is no single underlying invariant of cognition.

## Possible displacement of the seed

The paper does not yet supply a missing operator. It does pressure us to distinguish **model-internal geometry** from **human-owned geometry** and to ask what makes a remapping coherent rather than merely convenient.
