# source — Hutchins 1991: organizing work by adaptation

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Edwin Hutchins, “Organizing Work by Adaptation,” *Organization Science* 2(1), 1991, 14–39. https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2.1.14  
Branch: `distributed-knowing`

## Native question

How can a work group reorganize itself after a sudden change in its informational environment when no designer has specified the new organization?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Hutchins studies a ship-navigation team after an engineering failure disabled important navigational equipment. The team searched through computational and social alternatives and settled into a new stable work configuration.

Crucially, parts of the new division of cognitive labor arose from individuals adapting to local task demands rather than from anyone centrally designing the solution. Hutchins argues that the solution was, in an important sense, discovered by the organization before it was represented and learned by the individual participants.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

A detailed real-world case analysis of navigation work under equipment failure. The argument follows the actual redistribution of information-processing tasks and the emergence of a stable configuration from local adaptations.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- One navigation case cannot establish a universal theory of organization or cognition.
- “The organization discovered” is a level-of-analysis claim; it does not by itself establish that the organization is a conscious or epistemic agent.
- Deliberate reflection still contributed to some aspects of the adaptation.

## Assumptions under pressure

Coherent system-level adaptation need not be preceded by an explicit global representation in any participant. A stable organization of work can emerge first and become individually representable afterward.

## Rival reading

The episode can be described without attributing cognition to the organization: individual local adaptations plus environmental selection may be sufficient. The system-level description may compress the process without naming a new agent.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** “where is the learner?” may not have a single answer. The locus where a useful configuration is selected can differ from the locus where that configuration is later represented or understood.
