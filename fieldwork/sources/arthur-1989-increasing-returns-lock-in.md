# source — Arthur 1989: increasing returns and lock-in

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: W. Brian Arthur, “Competing Technologies, Increasing Returns, and Lock-In by Historical Events,” *The Economic Journal* 99(394), 1989, 116–131. https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecj/econjl/v99y1989i394p116-31.html  
Branch: `path-dependence-and-lock-in`

## Native question

How can competing technologies become locked into one trajectory because early adoption events change later incentives under increasing returns?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Arthur models technology choice when adoption itself improves a technology's attractiveness. Under increasing returns, small or random historical events can be amplified, causing one technology to dominate. The selected outcome can be difficult to predict ex ante, difficult to reverse, and not necessarily the globally most efficient alternative.

The important mechanism is positive feedback: earlier choices change the payoff landscape facing later choices.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The paper develops formal stochastic models of technology adoption and derives conditions under which increasing returns produce multiple possible long-run outcomes and lock-in.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- Not every persistence phenomenon is path dependence; the mechanism requires self-reinforcing effects.
- Real technology markets may retain entry, substitution and institutional interventions absent from simplified models.
- “Inefficient lock-in” is conditional on the model's welfare and comparison assumptions.

## Assumptions under pressure

The future option set is not experienced against a fixed landscape. A trajectory can modify the relative attractiveness and reversibility of later transitions.

## Rival reading

Some apparent lock-in can instead reflect hidden quality differences, rational switching costs, or changing external conditions. Historical sequence matters only when a mechanism transmits history into current payoffs.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** state-space exploration is potentially path-dependent. Exploration order can alter later reachability, not just our knowledge of a pre-existing frontier. The research system therefore needs resets and genuinely blind branches not only for epistemic diversity but to counter self-reinforcing search trajectories.
