# source — Grossman & Stiglitz 1980: impossibility of informationally efficient markets

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Sanford J. Grossman & Joseph E. Stiglitz, “On the Impossibility of Informationally Efficient Markets,” *American Economic Review* 70(3), 1980, 393–408. https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v70y1980i3p393-408.html  
Branch: `price-mediated-information`

## Native question

Can market prices fully reveal costly private information while still giving participants an incentive to acquire that information?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Grossman and Stiglitz show a fundamental tension in models of informational efficiency. If prices perfectly reveal all costly information, an uninformed trader can free-ride on prices and has no reason to pay to become informed. But if nobody pays to become informed, the information cannot be fully incorporated into prices.

The model therefore supports an equilibrium degree of informational imperfection: prices aggregate some information but cannot, under the modeled incentives, be perfectly revealing.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The contribution is a formal economic model. The conclusion follows from the incentives created by costly information acquisition and competitive price formation, not from a general empirical law that all real markets have the same efficiency level.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- The result depends on the model's assumptions about information cost, preferences, market structure and equilibrium.
- Real markets contain institutions, regulation and strategic behavior not exhausted by the model.
- A price is one public signal; markets transmit information through other channels too.
- Partial informational efficiency is not the same as social optimality.

## Assumptions under pressure

An aggregate representation can change the behavior of the agents that generate the represented information. Better public compression can reduce the incentive to maintain the information supply.

## Rival reading

The result is not an argument against aggregation; it explains why useful aggregation may require residual opacity and rents to information production.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** representation and world cannot always be modeled as one-way. A public summary may feed back into the process that generates its inputs, creating an **endogenous limit to compression or observability**.
