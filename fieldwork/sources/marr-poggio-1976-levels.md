# source — Marr & Poggio 1976: from understanding computation to understanding neural circuitry

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: David Marr & Tomaso Poggio, “From Understanding Computation to Understanding Neural Circuitry,” MIT AI Memo 357 / Neurosciences Research Program Bulletin 15(3), 1976. Public repository copy: http://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/5782  
Branch: `levels-of-explanation`

## Native question

At what distinct descriptive levels must an information-processing system be understood, and how are those levels constrained by the problem and by the available physical mechanisms?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Marr and Poggio argue that the central nervous system should be understood at multiple nearly independent levels: the computation/problem, the algorithms implementing it, the mechanisms to which algorithms are committed, and the physical realization of those mechanisms.

The problem to be solved constrains the computation; available hardware constrains mechanisms; algorithms are selected under both kinds of constraint. A complete account therefore cannot be replaced simply by a description at one level.

Later formulations often compress this into three levels—computational, algorithmic/representational, and implementational—but the 1976 memo is useful because it makes the separation between algorithm and mechanism explicit.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The memo develops the methodological framework through examples of theories at different levels in visual and neural processing.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- The framework does not guarantee clean independence between levels in every system.
- Later work has argued for stronger cross-level integration, embodiment, and resource constraints.
- Calling two claims “different levels” can become a way of avoiding genuine conflict unless the mapping between levels is made explicit.

## Rival reading

The levels may be best understood as methodological abstraction barriers rather than natural joints in reality. Some systems may resist the decomposition, and some explanatory questions may require cross-level coupling from the start.

## Possible displacement

**Candidate displacement:** cross-domain regularities must be indexed by explanatory level. A functional pattern shared across systems does not by itself establish a shared algorithm, mechanism, embodiment, or ontology.
