# source — Argote & Guo 2016: routines and transactive memory

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Linda Argote & Jerry M. Guo, “Routines and transactive memory systems: Creating, coordinating, retaining, and transferring knowledge in organizations,” *Research in Organizational Behavior* 36 (2016), 65–84. DOI: 10.1016/j.riob.2016.10.002. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.riob.2016.10.002  
Branch: `institutional-continuity`

## Native question

How do organizations create, coordinate, retain, and transfer knowledge across time, especially as people change?

## Claims in the source's own terms

The review compares two organizational knowledge repositories.

**Routines** are repeated, interdependent patterns of action that capture past experience and support consistent future performance. They are relatively independent of particular members, can be resilient to member turnover, and can sometimes transfer across organizations. They can create inertia but can also enable change.

**Transactive memory systems (TMSs)** are systems of “who knows what”: specialized knowledge remains in individual members while transactive processes let the group locate and coordinate that expertise. TMSs are more dependent on the identities of members, more vulnerable to turnover, and particularly useful for innovation and uncertainty.

The two can interact: routines can seed TMSs, and TMSs can crystallize into routines.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

This is a synthetic review of organizational-learning research rather than one field experiment. Its evidence is the accumulated literature comparing how routines and TMSs form, change, retain knowledge, transfer, and respond to turnover.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- “Knowledge repository” is an organizational-science construct, not a claim that an organization literally has a mind.
- Resilience to turnover is relative and conditional, not permanent identity under arbitrary change.
- The paper compares two mechanisms and explicitly calls for more research on their interrelationship.

## Assumptions under pressure

Organizational continuity need not mean persistence of the same people or the same location of knowledge. Different forms of memory have different dependencies on substrate membership.

## Rival reading

Observed persistence can also be attributed to documents, incentives, technologies, hiring practices, legal constraints, or environmental regularities rather than routines or TMSs alone. The review's categories are useful explanatory lenses, not exhaustive ontology.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** distinguish persistence carried by **repeatable action patterns** from persistence carried by **a network that knows where specialized state resides**. “Memory” and “identity” may have multiple mechanisms with different turnover tolerances; one invariant vocabulary is too coarse.
