# source — Szathmáry 2015: major evolutionary transitions theory 2.0

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Eörs Szathmáry, “Toward major evolutionary transitions theory 2.0,” *PNAS* 112(33), 2015, 10104–10111. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1421398112. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4547294/  
Branch: `transitions-in-individuality`

## Native question

What recurring processes turn lower-level evolutionary units into higher-level individuals, and how should major transitions be decomposed?

## Claims in the source's own terms

The paper revises major-transitions theory around two dimensions: changes in informational systems and changes in individuality. It decomposes transitions into phases of **origin, maintenance, and transformation** of higher-level units rather than treating a transition as a single jump.

It distinguishes fraternal transitions among similar lower-level units from egalitarian transitions among unlike units and emphasizes multilevel selection as a recurring explanatory mechanism.

No claim is made that all transitions share every feature with identical weight.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

This is a conceptual synthesis of two decades of theoretical and empirical work on major transitions. It reorganizes the canonical transition list and uses comparative recurrence across cases to motivate a revised framework.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- The framework does not supply one universal necessary-and-sufficient test for individuality across every case.
- Different transitions have different mechanisms and histories.
- “Information” is not by itself the whole explanation; cooperation, conflict, ecological conditions, and selection levels matter.

## Assumptions under pressure

The unit that counts as an individual can itself be an evolutionary outcome. The explanatory boundary need not be fixed in advance.

## Rival reading

Some apparent major transitions may be better explained case-by-case rather than as instances of a unified transition theory. Broad transition categories can hide mechanistic differences.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** “the learner” or relevant system boundary may be historically produced rather than given. A research framework that asks where adaptation or error integration occurs may need to allow the unit itself to change across a process.
