# source — BIPM/JCGM VIM3: metrological traceability

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Joint Committee for Guides in Metrology, *International Vocabulary of Metrology (VIM3)*, entries 2.26, 2.41, 2.42. https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.41.html  
Branch: `metrological-traceability`

## Native question

What makes a measurement result traceable to a common reference, and what information must travel with that relation?

## Claims in the source's own terms

VIM3 defines **metrological traceability** as a property of a measurement result whereby it can be related to a reference through a documented unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to measurement uncertainty.

A traceability chain is therefore not just a provenance list. It is a sequence of standards and calibrations that relates a result to a reference, while uncertainty accumulates through the chain.

VIM3 separately defines measurement uncertainty as a parameter characterizing the dispersion of quantity values attributed to a measurand based on the information used.

The notes explicitly warn that traceability alone does not guarantee that uncertainty is adequate for a particular purpose or that mistakes are absent.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

This is a standards vocabulary rather than an empirical paper. Its force comes from the formalized metrological practice used to make measurement results comparable across instruments and laboratories.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- Traceability is not accuracy by itself.
- A documented chain can exist while uncertainty is too large for the intended decision.
- Traceability does not eliminate mistakes.
- The framework presupposes a specified reference, measurement model, and calibration hierarchy.

## Assumptions under pressure

A physical interaction with the world is not yet a comparable measurement result. Reliability depends on the transformations that connect the interaction to a reference and on explicit accounting for uncertainty.

## Rival reading

Metrology is a specialized institutional practice. Its calibration-chain requirements should not automatically be generalized to every kind of epistemic contact, especially qualitative or exploratory inquiry.

## Possible displacement of the working backbone

**Candidate displacement:** “consequential error must survive a channel” may need a stronger formulation: the channel must preserve enough relation to a reference for the error to be interpretable, and its uncertainty must be known well enough for the decision at hand.
