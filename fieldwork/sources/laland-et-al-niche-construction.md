# source — Laland et al.: niche construction and sources of selection

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Kevin N. Laland and collaborators on niche construction, including “Niche construction, sources of selection and trait coevolution,” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* (2017). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5566808/  
Branch: `niche-construction-feedback`

## Native question

How do organism-induced environmental changes alter subsequent natural selection and evolutionary trajectories?

## Claims in the source's own terms

Niche construction refers to organisms modifying or choosing components of their environments in ways that can alter ecological processes and selection pressures.

The paper argues that repeated, directed environmental modification can impose a statistical bias on selection and thereby influence trait coevolution. Related work describes ecological inheritance: descendants encounter environmental conditions partly shaped by prior organisms.

The broader niche-construction literature treats this as feedback between organisms and selective environments rather than a one-way picture in which organisms only adapt to externally fixed conditions.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The literature combines formal population-genetic models, comparative examples, and empirical programs. Earlier models show that even modest environmental modification can change fixation, polymorphism, and evolutionary dynamics; later work derives testable predictions about trait coevolution and sources of selection.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- It is disputed whether niche construction deserves status as a distinct evolutionary process or is better treated as environmental modification already representable in standard evolutionary theory.
- Not every environmental change is evolutionarily important; scale, duration, and effect on selection matter.
- Adaptive niche construction and incidental environmental modification should not be conflated.

## Assumptions under pressure

The environment relevant to future adaptation need not be exogenous or fixed. System behavior can alter the transition/payoff structure faced later by itself or descendants.

## Rival reading

Critics accept that organisms modify environments but argue that standard evolutionary theory can already model the resulting changes in fitness and selection without elevating niche construction to a separate process.

## Possible displacement of the working backbone

**Candidate displacement:** “history shapes accessibility” may be stronger than path dependence among pre-existing options. In some systems, action changes the environmental conditions that define later selection pressures, so exploration can partly construct its own future search landscape.
