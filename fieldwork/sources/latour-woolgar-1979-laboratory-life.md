# source — Latour & Woolgar 1979/1986: Laboratory Life

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Bruno Latour & Steve Woolgar, *Laboratory Life: The Construction of Scientific Facts* (original 1979; 2nd ed. Princeton University Press, 1986). https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt32bbxc  
Branch: `mediated-scientific-contact`

## Native question

How do everyday laboratory practices, instruments, records, papers, and social interactions transform uncertain observations into statements that become treated as scientific facts?

## Claims in the source's own terms

The book presents an ethnographic account of scientific practice inside a neuroendocrinology laboratory. It emphasizes the role of **inscription devices** and written traces: phenomena become transportable and arguable through graphs, measurements, records, drafts, and papers rather than by an investigator simply confronting an unmediated object.

Latour and Woolgar track how statements lose qualifiers as evidence and social support accumulate, eventually becoming sufficiently stabilized that the work required to construct and defend them disappears from ordinary view.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The argument is based on extended ethnographic observation at Roger Guillemin's Salk Institute laboratory, including laboratory routines, publication practices, credibility, instrumentation, and scientific argument.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- The book is an ethnography of scientific practice, not a controlled demonstration that external reality plays no constraining role.
- “Construction” has strong and weak readings; later realist and Bayesian accounts can agree that evidence is mediated while rejecting stronger social-constructivist conclusions.
- Laboratory mediation does not imply arbitrariness: instruments and experiments can still fail, surprise, and constrain claims.

## Rival reading

The same observed process may be described as progressive evidential stabilization: instrumentation and social criticism are mechanisms for extracting reliable information about a mind-independent world rather than constituting the fact itself.

## Possible displacement

**Candidate displacement:** contact is not a primitive event. An answer from the world typically arrives through a **measurement / inscription / transport / verification channel** whose structure determines what errors can return and how far they can propagate.
