# source — Hoel, Albantakis & Tononi 2013: causal emergence

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Erik P. Hoel, Larissa Albantakis & Giulio Tononi, “Quantifying causal emergence shows that macro can beat micro,” *PNAS* 110(49), 2013, 19790–19795. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1314922110. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3856819/  
Branch: `coarse-graining-and-universality`

## Native question

Can a macro-level description be more causally informative than the complete micro-level description from which it is derived?

## Claims in the source's own terms

The paper defines effective information for causal models in terms that depend on state-space size, selectivity, determinism, and degeneracy. In constructed examples, effective information can be higher at a macro spatiotemporal scale even though the macro mechanism is completely specified by the micro mechanism.

The claim is not that the macro violates microphysical determination; it is that a coarse-graining can reduce causal noise/degeneracy enough to yield a more informative causal model under the chosen measure.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The authors construct simple causal systems, calculate effective information at micro and macro scales, and exhibit cases where the macro description scores higher.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- The result depends on the formal causal measure and choice of interventions/coarse-graining.
- It does not prove that macro causes are universally more real or useful.
- Not every coarse-graining produces causal emergence.

## Assumptions under pressure

Discarding microscopic distinctions can sometimes increase, not merely preserve, causal informativeness for a specified analysis.

## Rival reading

The improvement may reflect the chosen representation and metric rather than a metaphysical emergence of new causal powers. The micro model still determines the macro behavior.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** a cut can do more than compress. Under some formal criteria it can improve effective causal discrimination by removing degeneracy. This differs from both task-relative information bottlenecks and RG relevance.
