# source — Montévil & Mossio 2015: biological organisation as closure of constraints

Status: **stressed**  
Provenance: Maël Montévil & Matteo Mossio, “Biological organisation as closure of constraints,” *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 372 (2015), 179–191. DOI: 10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029.  
Branch: `organizational-closure`

## Native question

How can biological self-determination be characterized in physical systems that remain thermodynamically open and continuously exchange matter and energy with their surroundings?

## Claims in the source's own terms

The authors distinguish two causal regimes:

- **processes** — transformations occurring in non-equilibrium open thermodynamic conditions;
- **constraints** — relatively conserved structures that channel those processes over a relevant time scale.

Biological organization is proposed to realize a **closure of constraints**: constraints mutually depend on one another for their maintenance while jointly acting on underlying processes. The organism is therefore not materially closed; the claimed closure is organizational and causal.

This framework is intended to characterize self-maintenance and to help draw boundaries between interacting biological systems.

## Evidence / argument actually offered

The paper develops a conceptual and formal characterization, including criteria for distinguishing constraints from processes, dependence relations among constraints, and toy organizational schemes. It links the framework to biological cases and to prior work on autonomy, but it is primarily theoretical rather than a demonstration that every biological phenomenon is best explained this way.

## Explicit limits / unknowns

- Closure of constraints is a high-level theoretical abstraction, not a complete organism model.
- Organisms remain thermodynamically open; “closure” must not be read as physical isolation.
- The account does not entail cognition, representation, goal states, or a Platonic source of form.
- A closure relation can be too permissive unless the constraint/process distinction and dependence criteria do real empirical work.

## Assumptions under pressure

Persistence can be explained through a network whose components maintain the conditions for one another's continued causal efficacy. That is different from saying the system is converging toward a stored target pattern.

## Rival reading

A mechanistic critic can treat closure as a useful systems-level description of an ordinary biochemical network and deny that it introduces a distinct causal regime. The framework earns explanatory force only if it supports distinctions, interventions, or boundary judgments that a lower-level description does not supply as effectively.

## Possible displacement of the seed

**Candidate displacement:** `closure` must split into at least two meanings in our work. **Organizational closure** can be what allows a form or capability to persist; **epistemic closure pressure** is the premature hardening of a belief or map. One can be generative while the other is dangerous.

More strongly, persistence may sometimes be generated by mutually maintained constraints rather than by an invariant target. That creates a rival account to target-state language without yet settling which applies where.
