# branch — intervention and identifiability

Role: **alien-cross-cutting**  
Status: open

## Native question

When can multiple models fit every available observation equally well, and what additional intervention makes them distinguishable?

## Why this branch exists

The problem is native to statistics, causal inference, inverse problems, and system identification. It gives a precise case where more passive observation cannot resolve uncertainty, while a deliberately chosen perturbation can change the information available.

## Native vocabulary / literatures

Identifiability, indistinguishability, latent variables, inverse problems, interventions, experimental design, causal structure, structural identifiability, observational equivalence.

## Competing explanations to preserve

- ambiguity is structural and cannot be removed by more data of the same kind;
- theoretical criteria such as simplicity can select among equivalent fits;
- interventions can sometimes break observational equivalence;
- some models remain non-identifiable even after practical interventions.

## What would make this branch matter

A principled promotion rule for choosing probes: when passive evidence leaves alternatives equivalent, prefer an intervention that makes their predictions diverge.

## Stop condition

Pause when the branch no longer changes how we design discriminators or when the remaining identifiability issues are domain-specific technicalities with no new research-system consequence.
