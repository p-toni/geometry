# branch — generative immune repertoire

Role: **alien**  
Status: open

## Native question

How can a finite organism recognize a vast and partly novel antigen space without storing one detector for every possible antigen?

## Why this branch exists

Adaptive immunity addresses coverage under extreme combinatorial uncertainty through repertoire generation, multispecific recognition, selection, amplification, tolerance, and memory rather than exhaustive enumeration.

## Native vocabulary / literatures

Clonal selection, V(D)J recombination, combinatorial diversity, junctional diversity, cross-reactivity, affinity maturation, self-tolerance, immune memory.

## Competing explanations to preserve

- repertoire generation plus clonal selection;
- cross-reactivity / multispecificity;
- innate pattern-recognition contributions;
- germline biases and constrained repertoire structure;
- tolerance mechanisms that trade coverage for self/non-self discrimination.

## What would make this branch matter to the seed

If broad coverage comes from a generative-and-selective process rather than carrying all possibilities, that is a concrete architecture for bounded systems facing spaces larger than their representational capacity.

## Stop condition

Pause when additional immunology only adds biochemical detail without changing the architecture of generation, coverage, selection, or discrimination.
