# branch — organisational closure in biology

Role: **rival**  
Status: open

## Native question

What makes a biological system self-maintaining and organizationally distinct while its material constituents and energetic inputs continuously change?

## Why this branch exists

Levin and Lee both use language of form, persistence, and closure, but biology already contains a developed autonomy tradition in which persistence is explained through mutually maintained constraints in thermodynamically open systems. That account deserves to be understood without translating it into our vocabulary first.

## Native vocabulary / literatures

Processes, constraints, closure of constraints, self-maintenance, thermodynamic openness, biological organization, autonomy, functional dependence, system boundaries.

## Competing explanations to preserve

- closure-of-constraints accounts;
- dynamical-systems and attractor accounts;
- mechanistic molecular accounts;
- evolutionary-developmental accounts;
- target-state/cybernetic accounts;
- stronger pattern-space accounts.

## What would make this branch matter to the seed

A principled distinction between persistence generated by mutually maintained causal organization and persistence explained by navigation toward a represented or externally specified target.

## Stop condition

If closure remains only a redescriptive vocabulary with no empirical or formal discriminator from ordinary mechanism, stop promoting it.
