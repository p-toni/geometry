# branch — ecological resilience

Role: **alien**  
Status: open

## Native question

What does it mean for an ecological system to persist when its state fluctuates, disturbances are large, and return to one equilibrium is not the relevant notion of success?

## Why this branch exists

Ecology developed a distinction between stability-as-return and resilience-as-persistence under disturbance. It is a native test of whether our existing persistence vocabulary is too tied to fixed outputs or restoration to a target.

## Native vocabulary / literatures

Resilience, stability, persistence, alternative stable states, basin of attraction, disturbance, ecological regime, self-organization, adaptive cycle.

## Competing explanations to preserve

- engineering stability: speed/variance of return near an equilibrium;
- ecological resilience: amount of disturbance a system can absorb before changing regime;
- persistence of relationships may matter more than constancy of state;
- later resilience literature contains multiple non-equivalent definitions.

## What would make this branch matter

A distinction between “return to the same state” and “remain the same kind of system through state change” that survives beyond ecology and changes a live collision.

## Stop condition

Pause when resilience vocabulary starts expanding faster than discriminating mechanisms or when the branch becomes generic metaphor rather than ecological explanation.
