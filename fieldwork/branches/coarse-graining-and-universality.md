# branch — coarse-graining and universality

Role: **alien / rival**  
Status: open

## Native question

Why can systems with very different microscopic details exhibit the same macroscopic behavior, and what information is discarded when moving between scales?

## Why this branch exists

Renormalization-group methods address scale change, universality, and coarse-graining in a mathematically explicit domain where many microscopic distinctions become irrelevant to large-scale behavior.

## Native vocabulary / literatures

Critical phenomena, universality classes, relevant and irrelevant operators, coarse-graining, fixed points, scaling exponents, renormalization-group flow.

## Competing explanations to preserve

- renormalization-group accounts of universality;
- effective field theory / scale separation;
- phenomenological coarse descriptions that do not require RG machinery;
- cases where microscopic details remain relevant and universality fails.

## What would make this branch matter to the seed

A principled account of when a difference may be discarded because it becomes irrelevant under scale transformation, rather than because an observer merely chose to ignore it.

## Stop condition

Pause if the branch becomes only a metaphor for abstraction or compression rather than an account of explicit scale transformations and testable universality.
