# branch — cultural transmission and structure

Role: **alien**  
Status: open

## Native question

How can repeated transmission through bounded learners transform an initially unstructured communication system into one that is easier to learn and more systematically organized?

## Why this branch exists

Iterated-learning experiments provide an empirical setting where structure can emerge across generations of transmission without a designer and without assuming that any one learner carries the whole historical process.

## Native vocabulary / literatures

Iterated learning, cultural evolution, compositionality, learnability, transmission bottlenecks, artificial languages, diffusion chains.

## Competing explanations to preserve

- transmission-driven adaptation of the language;
- individual cognitive biases;
- communicative pressure;
- experimental-task artifacts;
- biological language-specific constraints.

## What would make this branch matter to the seed

Evidence that boundedness and repeated transmission can create structured representations rather than merely degrade them, especially when no participant intends the resulting global structure.

## Stop condition

Pause if the branch becomes a generic claim that 'compression creates structure' without preserving the experimental distinction between learning, use, transmission, and selection.
