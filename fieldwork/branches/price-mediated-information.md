# branch — price-mediated information

Role: **alien / rival**  
Status: open

## Native question

How can dispersed private information become partially reflected in a public price when no participant possesses the whole information set?

## Why this branch exists

Market microstructure and information economics study aggregation under strategic incentives. They therefore provide a case where a public summary can be useful precisely because information remains distributed—and where making the summary too informative can undermine the incentives that produce it.

## Native vocabulary / literatures

Dispersed knowledge, price discovery, informed trading, noise trading, informational efficiency, information acquisition incentives, market makers, order flow.

## Competing explanations to preserve

- Hayekian price coordination under dispersed knowledge;
- strategic microstructure models such as Kyle;
- Grossman–Stiglitz limits on full informational efficiency;
- institutional and regulatory effects on who acquires and reveals information.

## What would make this branch matter to the seed

If aggregation changes the incentives that generate its inputs, then a representation is not merely a passive compression of an external world. The summary can alter the process that keeps the summary informative.

## Stop condition

Pause when additional papers only restate the information-efficiency tradeoff without changing the endogenous feedback between acquisition, aggregation, and public signal quality.
