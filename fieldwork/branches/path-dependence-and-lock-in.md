# branch — path dependence and lock-in

Role: **alien / hostile-to-neutral-search**  
Status: open

## Native question

How do small early contingencies become amplified into durable trajectories that change which future options are attractive, reachable or reversible?

## Why this branch exists

Path-dependence research studies systems in which transition history changes the future state space through increasing returns, installed bases, learning, coordination effects or institutional commitment. It is a direct test of any exploration architecture that treats candidate states as fixed and independently visitable.

## Native vocabulary / literatures

Increasing returns, positive feedback, lock-in, multiple equilibria, historical contingency, non-ergodicity, switching costs, self-reinforcement.

## Competing explanations to preserve

- increasing-returns lock-in;
- ordinary switching costs without deep path dependence;
- rational adaptation to changing payoffs;
- cases where apparent lock-in disappears under broader market or institutional analysis.

## What would make this branch matter to the seed

If exploration changes future accessibility, then search is not a neutral walk over a static state space. The order of inquiry can become part of the causal system being studied.

## Stop condition

Pause when additional examples merely label persistence as “path dependence” without identifying a mechanism that makes earlier states alter later transition probabilities or payoffs.
