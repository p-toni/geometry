# branch — mathematical geometry in its own terms

Role: **adjacent**  
Status: open

## Native question

What has counted as geometry inside mathematics as the field moved from axioms and coordinates to transformations, manifolds, topology, connections, and bundles?

## Why this branch exists

We are using `geometry` as an operational word in a broader epistemic project. Before deciding whether that use is substantive or metaphorical, we need a control branch that learns how mathematicians themselves have changed the object and methods called geometry.

This branch is allowed to use geometry vocabulary because geometry itself is the native domain.

## Native vocabulary / literatures

Synthetic and axiomatic geometry; analytic geometry; transformation groups; invariants; differential geometry; manifolds; local versus global structure; topology; connections; curvature; fiber bundles; characteristic classes.

## Competing explanations to preserve

- geometry as the invariant theory of a transformation group;
- geometry as structures defined on manifolds;
- geometry as local-to-global organization;
- geometry as relational/structural mathematics with no single essence;
- the possibility that `geometry` is historically plural rather than reducible to one definition.

## What would make this branch matter to the seed

A native mathematical distinction that tells us when our broader use of `geometry` has enough structure to do explanatory work and when it is only evocative language.

## Stop condition

If the branch becomes a history-of-mathematics tour without producing discriminating criteria for our use of the word, stop.
