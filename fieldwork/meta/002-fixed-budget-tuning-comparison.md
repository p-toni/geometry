# meta-test 002 — fixed-budget tuning comparison

Status: **equal-evidence procedural comparison; not externally independent**

Question: **Why must fixed-pitch tuning systems compromise among pure intervals, and what does 12-tone equal temperament optimize?**

## Fixed budget

Both arms receive the same evidence packet and no additional retrieval:

1. H. Broer et al., “The Mathematical Structure of Pythagorean-like Tuning Systems,” *The Mathematical Intelligencer* (2025): Pythagorean comma, meantone, irregular temperaments, equal temperament;
2. C. H. S. Vieira et al., “Geometric Construction of Pythagorean and Just Musical Scales and Commas,” *The Mathematical Intelligencer* (2022): exact ratio construction and Pythagorean comma;
3. C. Scholtz, “Algorithms for Tunings and Temperaments,” *Music Theory Online* 4.4 (1998): Pythagorean tuning and equal-temperament construction.

Same model family, same evidence, same maximum output size. The only manipulated variable is procedure.

## Arm A — simple baseline

Procedure:

```text
read packet → answer question clearly
```

Recovered answer:

- Pure octaves have ratio 2:1 and pure fifths 3:2.
- Twelve pure fifths do not equal seven octaves: `(3/2)^12 / 2^7` is the Pythagorean comma.
- A closed 12-note fixed-pitch system therefore cannot keep every fifth pure while also identifying enharmonic notes and closing exactly at the octave.
- Historical temperaments distribute the discrepancy differently.
- 12-tone equal temperament spreads the comma across the fifths and makes all semitones equal, enabling transposition/modulation across keys at the cost of making most intervals slightly impure.

Strength: compact, correct, useful.

## Arm B — anti-collapse procedure

Procedure:

```text
native claim → separate mechanisms / objectives → preserve alternatives → ask what would discriminate “best”
```

Recovered distinctions:

1. **Arithmetic incompatibility** — exact integer-ratio generators do not close simultaneously under the chosen finite pitch-class identification.
2. **Closure rule** — deciding that enharmonic spellings collapse to the same key is itself part of the representation.
3. **Optimization target** — Pythagorean, meantone, well temperament, just intonation, and equal temperament preserve different interval relationships.
4. **Use regime** — a tuning that is excellent for a limited key set can be poor for unrestricted modulation.
5. **Perceptual / timbral objective** — mathematical ratio error is not identical to perceived consonance or musical usefulness.
6. **Instrument constraint** — fixed-pitch keyboards face a different problem from voices or continuously tunable instruments.

The phrase “equal temperament is optimal” therefore has no content until a loss function / use regime is fixed.

## Discriminator generated by the protocol

Hold fixed:

- 12 pitch classes;
- octave equivalence;
- a specified repertoire of intervals and keys.

Predeclare a loss function over interval error and modulation coverage. Compare candidate temperaments under that loss. Then change only the loss weights.

If the preferred temperament changes, “best tuning” is objective-indexed rather than an intrinsic total ordering.

## Frozen criteria comparison

| Criterion | Simple baseline | Anti-collapse | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct mechanisms | arithmetic + historical compromise | arithmetic, representation, objective, use regime, perception, instrument | protocol advantage |
| Unsupported universalization | avoids major overclaim | explicitly blocks unindexed “optimal” | protocol advantage |
| Rival explanations | names alternatives | preserves why alternatives win under different objectives | protocol advantage |
| Failure conditions | implicit | explicit loss-function discriminator | protocol advantage |
| Probe generation | none | yes | protocol advantage |
| Source/inference boundary | good | good | tie |
| Calibration | good | stronger around “best” | small protocol advantage |
| Evidence/search cost | equal by construction | equal retrieval, higher reasoning/artifact overhead | baseline cheaper in reasoning |

## Result

The first comparison's protocol advantage survives an **equal evidence budget** in this case. The gain again comes mainly from preserving objective dependence and generating a discriminator, not from retrieving more facts.

But the advantage is smaller than in a multi-mechanism scientific question. The baseline is already sufficient for most explanatory uses.

## What this does not establish

- Same model family generated both arms.
- Output-time / token cost was not perfectly matched, only evidence/retrieval budget.
- One mathematically clean problem is not a representative benchmark.
- The evaluator is not independent of either arm.

## Updated protocol hypothesis

> Anti-collapse is most valuable when an apparently singular question hides multiple objectives, mechanisms, boundaries, or verification regimes. Its benefit should be measured against the extra reasoning overhead, not source count alone.
