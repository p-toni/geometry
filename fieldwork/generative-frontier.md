# generative frontier

Status: **protocol correction under test**

`State-space exploration` is an operating metaphor, not a commitment that every research problem comes with a fixed list of states or even a fixed set of coordinates.

Wave F exposed a stronger failure mode than path dependence:

> The system may not merely hide states from us. Inquiry may need to invent or recognize new observables, categories, variables, or dimensions before the later possibility can even be described.

## Four forms of frontier change

```text
1. unknown state, known coordinates
2. changed reachability among known states
3. changed transition rules / constraints
4. new coordinates / observables become necessary
```

The first three can still be modeled inside an inherited state description. The fourth changes the description itself.

## Why this matters for research design

Blind scouts and fresh starts reduce lock-in inside an inherited representation, but they do not guarantee category invention. A wide search can still be wide only in the dimensions we already know how to name.

The protocol therefore needs an explicit **coordinate-challenge** operation.

A coordinate challenge asks:

- Which variables are we assuming exist before the search begins?
- Which distinctions are impossible to express in the current schema?
- Did a historical discovery require a new measurable quantity, category, or unit of analysis?
- Are we calling something “unexplored” when the real problem is that our representation cannot state it?
- Would a source written entirely in another discipline force us to add a dimension rather than another value on an existing dimension?

## Operating rule

At least once per large round, ask a hostile investigator to challenge the **coordinates of the frontier**, not merely its coverage.

If the current representation cannot state the strongest counterexample without adding a new variable, that is a progress unit.

## Limit

This does not establish that reality has ontologically unprestatable phase spaces. It only prevents the research protocol from assuming that its current coordinates are complete.
