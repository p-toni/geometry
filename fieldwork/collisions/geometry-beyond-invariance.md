# collision — geometry beyond invariance

Status: **open**

## Independent inputs

- `chern-1990-what-is-geometry` — the mathematical field called geometry repeatedly changes its organizing machinery: axioms, coordinates, calculus, transformation groups, local manifolds, topology, connections, bundles.
- `reck-schiemer-2025-mathematical-structuralism` — mathematical structural practice does not uniquely determine a metaphysics of independently existing structures.
- `geometry-as-metaphor` — our hostile branch asks whether `geometry` in the corpus does explanatory work or merely redescribes relations suggestively.

## Why this is a collision rather than an agreement

Our seed currently places invariants and symmetries near the start of the explanatory chain. Klein's transformation-group conception makes that look mathematically natural—but Chern's history makes clear that Klein is one major stage, not the end of geometry. Riemannian locality, topological globalization, connection, transport, and curvature add kinds of structure that are not captured by simply saying “find invariants.”

At the same time, structuralism blocks the easy rescue in which all of these are declared manifestations of one pre-existing abstract geometry.

## New distinction / contradiction

We should keep at least these senses apart:

1. **geometry as invariance** — what survives a specified transformation group;
2. **geometry as local structure** — what can be defined in neighborhoods without privileged global coordinates;
3. **geometry as global gluing** — how local pieces form a manifold/topological whole;
4. **geometry as transport/connection** — how information or orientation is compared across locations;
5. **geometry as metaphor** — any relational structure merely described spatially.

The first four are mathematically constrained. The fifth may or may not be.

## Pressure on the seed

“Invariants induce symmetries” can no longer carry the burden of defining the geometry we care about. Even inside mathematics, invariance is one organizing principle among several connected developments.

This does not yet tell us to abandon the word. It tells us that `geometry` must eventually earn a more specific operational meaning.

## What remains unresolved

Whether there is a weak mathematical core broad enough to connect the first four without becoming vacuous, and whether our essays actually instantiate any of those cores rather than borrowing their language.

## Candidate discriminating probe

Take five concrete uses of `geometry` from the current essays. For each, require explicit answers to:

- what is the space or set of possible states?
- what counts as a neighborhood or adjacency?
- what transformations are allowed?
- what is preserved?
- can structure be transported or compared across states?
- what empirical/formal consequence follows that would not follow from saying merely “relations” or “structure”?

If the final question repeatedly has no answer, `geometry` is functioning as metaphor rather than mechanism.
