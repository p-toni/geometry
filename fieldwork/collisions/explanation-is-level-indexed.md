# collision — explanation is level-indexed

Status: **open**

## Independently formed inputs

- `levels-of-explanation` — computational, algorithmic, mechanistic, and physical descriptions answer different questions;
- `active-matter-collective-order` — similar macroscopic order can arise from different microscopic interactions and conservation structure;
- `coarse-graining-and-universality` — large-scale regularities can be insensitive to many microscopic details in a specified regime;
- `universal-framework-critique` — broad descriptive fit can outrun explanatory discrimination.

## Displacement

A cross-domain regularity must be tagged by **what level it claims to unify**.

For example, two systems may share:

- a functional input/output relation while using different algorithms;
- a macroscopic universality class while differing in mechanism and history;
- an algorithm while using different physical implementations;
- a verbal description while sharing none of the above.

“Same structure” is therefore incomplete unless the explanatory level and preserved relations are stated.

## Pressure on fieldwork

The anti-collapse protocol should treat movement between explanatory levels as a provenance event. A source may support a functional analogy without licensing a mechanistic or ontological claim.

## Candidate verification contract

A later probe can take two simple systems with identical input/output behavior but provably different internal transition graphs and test whether a proposed cross-domain claim survives after its level is made explicit.

## What remains unresolved

Some theories deliberately explain by connecting levels rather than keeping them independent. The collision should not harden Marr's decomposition into a universal ontology.
