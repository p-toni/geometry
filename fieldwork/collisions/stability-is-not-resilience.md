# collision — stability is not resilience

Status: **open**

## Independent inputs

- `holling-1973-resilience-stability` — ecological persistence can concern surviving disturbance and remaining within a regime, not returning quickly to one equilibrium state.
- `mccolgan-difrisco-2024-developmental-system-drift` — phenotype can remain conserved while underlying developmental mechanisms drift.
- `montevil-mossio-2015-closure-of-constraints` — biological organization can be described through mutually maintained constraints in an open system.

## Why this is a collision rather than an agreement

The sources preserve different things: state, phenotype, regime, relation, or constraint organization. A system can be stable in one sense and non-resilient in another, or preserve an output while its mechanism changes.

## New distinction / contradiction

Separate:

- **state stability** — remain near / return to a state;
- **output persistence** — preserve a recognizable result;
- **regime resilience** — absorb disturbance without crossing into another regime;
- **organizational persistence** — preserve relations/constraints that sustain the system;
- **mechanism persistence** — preserve the underlying implementation.

## Pressure on the seed

An “invariant” can be cheap if it only names a stable output. We need to ask what perturbation class the system survives and which deeper organization is allowed to move.

## What remains unresolved

Whether counterfactual response under perturbation can provide a common test without flattening the domain-specific meanings of resilience and robustness.

## Candidate discriminating probe

Compare two systems with the same observed output but different mechanisms across a perturbation chosen to expose their different response basins. Ask whether “same invariant” survives the perturbation.
