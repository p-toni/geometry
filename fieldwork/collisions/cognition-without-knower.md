# collision — cognition without a knower

Status: **open**

## Independent inputs

- `hutchins-1991-organizing-work-by-adaptation` — a distributed work system can reach a useful organization before any participant explicitly represents the whole solution.
- `giere-2007-distributed-cognition-without-knowing` — distributed cognitive processing does not by itself justify attributing knowledge, belief, mind, or epistemic agency to the whole system.
- `openai-2026-navier-stokes` — a live case in which large-scale distributed AI search, formalization, and verification produce a mathematical artifact that no single search agent generated end-to-end.

## Why this is a collision rather than an agreement

Hutchins expands the useful unit of cognitive analysis beyond the individual. Giere accepts that expansion while blocking a slide from distributed processing to distributed knowing. The current AI case then makes the distinction practical: a system can apparently produce an epistemically valuable artifact while the location of understanding, verification, entitlement, and responsibility remains unsettled.

## New distinction / contradiction

The old question “where is the learner?” may be too singular. We should track several boundaries independently:

1. **transformation boundary** — which components jointly produce the result;
2. **memory boundary** — where state persists across steps;
3. **error-integration boundary** — where failed expectations alter future behavior;
4. **verification boundary** — what system checks the result against independent constraints;
5. **epistemic-subject boundary** — who is entitled to claim knowledge;
6. **responsibility boundary** — who answers for error or action.

They need not coincide.

## Pressure on the seed

“A bounded learner” may remain useful only if `learner` is not assumed to denote the same unit for every operation. The relevant boundary may change depending on whether we are asking about search, adaptation, verification, understanding, or responsibility.

This also pressures source-closed reconstruction as a universal ownership test: a larger system may be reliable precisely because no component can reconstruct the whole.

## What remains unresolved

Whether an epistemic system can legitimately be called a knower without person-like agency; whether formal verification can support knowledge that no individual understands end-to-end; and what minimum local reconstruction is required for responsible reliance.

## Candidate discriminating probe

Trace one theorem-level claim from the OpenAI Navier–Stokes case through search, synthesis, Lean formalization, independent checking, and expert uptake. At each stage, record which of the six boundaries above actually changes.

The probe succeeds if it lets us say something more precise than either “the AI knows” or “only a human can know.”
