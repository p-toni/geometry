# collision — representation can change its source

Status: **open**

## Independent inputs

- `grossman-stiglitz-1980-informational-efficiency` — a public price that fully reveals costly private information destroys the incentive to acquire that information, so the signal can undermine its own source.
- `kirby-cornish-smith-2008-iterated-learning` — representations passed through repeated learners are transformed by the constraints of transmission and learning; the output becomes the next generation's input.
- `openai-2026-navier-stokes` / the fieldwork process itself — published/formalized artifacts alter what later agents and reviewers inspect, search and attempt to verify.

## Why this is a collision rather than an agreement

Market prices, transmitted languages and research artifacts are not the same mechanism. But each breaks a simple map/world separation: the representation is fed back into the process that generates future evidence or structure.

## New distinction / contradiction

Separate:

- **descriptive representation** — summarizes a source treated as exogenous;
- **performative representation** — changes incentives or behavior in the source system;
- **iterative representation** — becomes input to the next reconstruction cycle;
- **selection-shaping representation** — changes which future possibilities get explored or amplified.

A representation can occupy several roles at once.

## Pressure on the seed

“The world answers” is too one-directional if the map changes the answering world. In some domains the loop is not map → action → feedback; the **map itself becomes an intervention** before any explicit action is taken.

## Pressure on fieldwork

Published collisions and branch summaries can make later scouts more likely to rediscover the same structure. This strengthens the case for context isolation and periodic fresh-start exploration.

## What remains unresolved

How to distinguish meaningful performativity from the trivial fact that any communicated information can affect someone. The criterion should require a measurable change in the source-generating process.

## Candidate discriminating probe

Take one research question and run two sequential scout chains. In one, each scout receives only the native question; in the other, each receives the prior scout's summary. Compare narrowing of sources, vocabulary and hypotheses across generations. Predeclare that increased convergence alone is not success: the question is whether inherited representation reduces independent discovery while increasing internal coherence.
