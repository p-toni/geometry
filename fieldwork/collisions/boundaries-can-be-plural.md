# collision — boundaries can be plural

Status: **open**

## Independently formed inputs

- `polycentric-governance` — authority, monitoring, rule-making, and correction can be distributed across overlapping centers;
- `distributed-knowing` — processing can be distributed even when epistemic entitlement is not attributed to one collective knower;
- `convergence-without-synchrony` — replicas can converge under local algebraic rules without a central coordinator;
- `collective-choice-without-comparison` — colony-level decisions can emerge from distributed recruitment and inhibition.

## Displacement

Asking “where is the boundary?” may presuppose that one boundary must carry all relevant functions.

A system can instead have different boundaries for:

- state / memory;
- observation;
- intervention;
- authority;
- error integration;
- verification;
- accountability;
- reproduction / persistence.

Those boundaries can overlap without coinciding.

## Pressure on fieldwork

When a branch claims a learner, agent, individual, or system, record **which operation requires that boundary** rather than treating system identity as primitive.

## Candidate discriminator

Look for cases where forcing all operations onto one boundary predicts failure or hides a mechanism that becomes explicit under plural boundaries. Polycentric governance and distributed computation provide candidates, but no probe is promoted yet.

## What remains unresolved

Plural boundaries may be a modeling convenience rather than a deep property. Some systems do have a privileged physical or legal boundary. The question is operational: which boundary is required for which claim?
