# comparison hygiene

This is an anti-collapse guard for cross-domain integration. It is **not** a universal ontology.

Before treating two native accounts as instances of one pattern, ask four questions.

## 1. What explanatory level is the claim on?

A resemblance may be functional, algorithmic, mechanistic, physical, institutional, historical, or something else.

Do not promote a shared functional pattern into a shared mechanism or ontology without an additional argument.

If the source does not fit a pre-existing level taxonomy, preserve its native decomposition rather than forcing it into one.

## 2. What causal role does the proposed structure play?

Keep separate unless contact licenses a merge:

- **epistemic partition** — groups distinctions in a representation;
- **admissibility constraint** — changes what states/transitions are permitted;
- **dynamical accessibility** — changes what is stable, likely, reachable, or costly under the dynamics;
- **historical accessibility** — prior trajectories change later possibilities/incentives.

These labels came from collisions in round 001. They are checks, not primitives of reality.

## 3. Which boundary does the claim require?

Do not assume one system boundary simultaneously owns memory, observation, intervention, verification, authority, error integration, accountability, and persistence.

Name the operation first; then ask what boundary carries it.

## 4. Through what channel does contact occur?

For claims that “the world answers,” trace enough of the path to locate failure:

```text
process → interaction/intervention → measurement/inscription
        → transport/transformation → verification → revision boundary
```

An external constraint can be real while the chosen channel remains unable to discriminate it.

## Use

A collision is stronger when it survives these questions. A collision that disappears after level, causal role, boundary, or channel is made explicit was probably semantic compression rather than discovery.
